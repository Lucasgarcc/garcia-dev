# Reasoning Before Code: Why AI Does Not Replace the Process

### The "Cliché" Nobody Follows
Understanding how the process of a Software (Product) works before wanting to start writing code is fundamental. Requirements and the target audience must be clearly defined. It may sound like a cliché, but this is extremely common in real software development, and many developers ignore it in their rush to code.

### The Market and Prompt Engineering
The market has changed significantly with the use of AI in development. This has created the need to adapt and start using **Prompt Engineering** inside the IDE, giving developers more productivity and faster delivery. However, here is the warning: fast delivery does not always mean efficient delivery.

### The Danger of Total Delegation
You must be very careful about completely delegating work to AI without understanding the process first. That is why my focus is to reinforce **Architecture and Processes**, limiting excessive AI use. The key is to delegate only simple tasks that remain under your **complete control** of the software.

A simple example is creating a **Design System** from scratch. You use the tool to accelerate the work, but the integrity of the software as a whole depends on your technical vision.

### Key Concepts in the Structural Layer
To maintain that integrity, you need to master the terms that form the foundation of modern development:

* **System Design:** The design of the solution: how systems communicate, how the database behaves and how the infrastructure supports the product. It is the master plan before the first line of code.
* **Smart Components:** Components that hold logic and state management. They are the "brain" that orchestrates data before passing it to the interface.

### The Golden Rule of a Pull Request
Never create a **PR (Pull Request)** without reviewing the code yourself, not through AI. The final word and the technical responsibility for what goes to production are yours. Knowledge prevails and prevents the loss of logical reasoning that the reckless use of these tools can cause.

> "Delegate execution, but never delegate understanding of the process."
