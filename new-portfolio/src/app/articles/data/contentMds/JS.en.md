# The Invisible Gap: Why Understanding the Fundamentals Is Your Greatest Advantage in the AI Era

### The Productivity Paradox and the Loss of Reasoning
In today's world, surrounded by AI and the ease of producing code with a few keystrokes, productivity seems to have never been higher. But there is a danger: the atrophy of logical reasoning. Knowledge must prevail; it is what keeps us from becoming mere "component copyists."

Understanding the fundamentals is not optional. No matter how much you study Vanilla JS, there is always a major difference between the raw foundation and a complex framework. There is a gap that the market overlooks, and it is where the true engineer stands out.

### What Do Frameworks Hide From You?
In this article, I share an observation from working with **Angular** and studying **React**. I noticed that, although they have very similar aspects — such as the constant use of methods and the classes \`constructor\` — the focus on how everything works at the structural layer remains quite vague.

Frameworks are often treated as "black boxes": you know what goes in and what comes out, but you do not understand the mechanism that supports rendering and state.

### Flux-framework: Bridging the Gap
My focus when creating a mini-framework (the **[Flux-framework](https://github.com/Lucasgarcc/Flux-framework)**) was precisely to demonstrate that it is possible to "bridge" this gap. The goal was to show that, with simple knowledge — but knowledge that requires a solid command of JavaScript — we can build our own abstractions.

By building this structural layer from scratch, you begin to realize that:
* **The Magic of the Constructor:** This is where the seed of state and the instance is truly planted.
* **DOM Manipulation:** Although abstracted, it is still about nodes, events and memory management.
* **Reactivity:** It can be reproduced with pure logic, bringing what you write in Vanilla closer to what Angular or React do "under the hood."

> "Artificial intelligence can generate the code, but logical reasoning and architectural decisions still belong to the engineer who masters the fundamentals."

### Conclusion: Knowledge Prevails
In a world saturated with abstractions, fundamental knowledge is what prevents the loss of logical reasoning. Creating something from scratch proves that a framework's structure is not mystical. Studying how things work *under the hood* is the only way to ensure that, regardless of the tool or the evolution of AI, you retain complete control over what you build.
